Every day, huge numbers of sentences need to be sorted into categories, like deciding whether a movie review is positive or negative. This process is called text classification, and it helps computers understand what people are saying.

Before this paper, people thought you needed complex models with many carefully designed features to understand language. But the question was whether a simple neural network could perform just as well.

Yoon Kim's idea was simple: turn each word into a vector, then use a convolutional neural network, or CNN, that slides over groups of neighboring words to find useful patterns. Finally, combine these patterns to predict the sentence's meaning or category.

This simple model beat the previous best on four out of seven benchmark tasks, including sentiment analysis and question classification. For example, on the MR movie review dataset, it achieved 81.0% accuracy using static word vectors. This showed that CNNs could recognize meaningful language patterns without requiring an overly complicated architecture.

The lesson is that simple models can achieve impressive results in natural language processing, as long as they learn useful patterns from the data. Kim's paper showed that CNNs, originally popular in image processing, could also work effectively with text.
